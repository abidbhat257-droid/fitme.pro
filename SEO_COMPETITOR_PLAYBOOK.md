# FitMe Pro SEO Competitor Playbook

## Purpose
Use this document as the standing SEO strategy for FitMe Pro. Competitor pages are research references for search intent, information architecture, keyword coverage, internal-link patterns, UX and content completeness. Never copy competitor wording, tables, illustrations or proprietary content.

## Primary direct competitors to benchmark
1. TDEE.co — connected calculator journey, medically reviewed positioning, formula transparency, goal-based calculations, strong internal linking between TDEE, BMR, calories, macros, protein and body fat.
2. FitnessCalculator.net — broad fitness calculator coverage, individual calculator landing pages, guides and FAQs.
3. DietXP — calculator hub organized by intent/category, method labels, evidence-informed positioning, pathways and supporting health content.
4. TDEE.TECH — calculator category hubs, formula/method explanations, related tools and educational blog content.
5. RepCalcs — fitness-performance calculator coverage and practical training use cases.
6. FitCalc — calculator pages supported by educational articles and fitness explanations.
7. SportyCalc — fitness calculator breadth across body metrics, calories, running, strength and heart rate.
8. Omni Calculator — benchmark for mature calculator-page depth, measurement instructions, formulas, examples, limitations, references and related tools.
9. CalculatorSoup — benchmark for clear worked examples, formulas, unit handling and explanatory search intent.
10. Verywell Fit — benchmark for health/fitness editorial depth, topic authority and trust signals rather than calculator UX alone.

## Competitor SEO patterns to learn from
- One strong search-intent page per calculator/topic.
- Exact topic in the title/H1 while keeping copy natural.
- Explain the metric before asking the user to interpret it.
- Show the formula or calculation method when appropriate.
- Give a worked example using realistic values.
- Explain every important input and how to measure it.
- Explain what the result means and what it does not mean.
- State limitations and sources instead of presenting estimates as facts.
- Link to complementary calculators to form topical clusters.
- Use category hubs to connect related calculators.
- Use FAQs for real search questions, not keyword stuffing.
- Support calculator pages with educational articles targeting informational queries.
- Add evidence/reference sections and an editorial methodology.
- Use trustworthy health sources and original research for health claims.

## Keyword strategy
Target three layers for each calculator:

### Core transactional
Examples: "BMI calculator", "TDEE calculator", "FFMI calculator", "waist to height ratio calculator", "running pace calculator".

### Modifier/long-tail
Examples: "BMI calculator kg cm", "TDEE calculator for weight loss", "protein calculator for muscle gain", "5K time predictor", "bench press 1RM calculator".

### Informational support
Examples: "what is FFMI", "how is TDEE calculated", "BMI limitations", "how to measure waist circumference", "pace vs speed".

Do not create thin pages just to capture modifiers. Prefer useful sections on the primary calculator page unless a modifier has distinct search intent that warrants its own page.

## Internal-linking model
Build deliberate journeys rather than isolated pages.

Body composition:
BMI → Body Fat → Lean Body Mass → FFMI/FMI → Waist-to-Height → BRI/ABSI → Ideal/Healthy Weight

Weight and calories:
BMR → TDEE → Maintenance Calories → Calorie Deficit/Surplus → Weight Loss/Gain Calories → Weight Timeline → Macro → Protein

Nutrition:
Daily Calories → Macros → Protein → Protein per Meal → Fiber → Water → Meal Macros

Running:
Pace → Speed → Pace-to-Speed → Training Pace → Splits → 5K → 10K → Half Marathon → Marathon → VO2 Max → Heart Rate Zones

Strength:
1RM → Rep Max → Bench/Squat/Deadlift 1RM → Strength Level → Strength-to-Weight → Training Volume → Wilks/DOTS

Heart:
Maximum HR → HRR → Target HR → HR Zones → Recovery → MAP → Pulse Pressure → Rate Pressure Product

Every calculator should link to relevant upstream/downstream tools and its category hub where appropriate.

## Blog/content strategy
Supporting content should answer questions that calculators cannot fully satisfy. Prioritize:
- definitions and explainers
- formula/methodology guides
- measurement guides
- comparisons between related metrics
- goal-based planning guides
- accuracy and limitations
- interpretation guides
- training and nutrition education

Every article should link naturally to one or more relevant calculators and receive links back from those calculators where useful.

## Backlink strategy
Do not pursue copied or spun content for links. Build linkable assets such as original calculation explainers, methodology pages, reference resources, useful comparison guides, calculators journalists/trainers can cite, and original aggregate insights from anonymized/non-sensitive public methodology—not user health data.

When backlink research is performed, record referring domains, anchor themes, linked pages and content format in a separate audit. Do not assume a competitor's backlink source is high quality merely because it links to them.

## Health SEO trust rules
- Use authoritative sources appropriate to the exact claim or formula.
- Prefer WHO, CDC, NIH/NIDDK, NHS, ACSM, professional organizations and the original peer-reviewed equation/model when relevant.
- Never invent a medical reviewer, credential or source.
- Distinguish estimates, screening tools and measured clinical values.
- Include limitations and a clear educational-use disclaimer.
- Avoid unsupported promises such as "most accurate" unless the evidence supports the wording.

## Current FitMe Pro priority
The 100-calculator architecture is the product foundation. Improve page quality before adding another large calculator set. Prioritize Ponderal Index, Body Density, Waist-to-Height Ratio, Obesity Class and TDEE because early Search Console impressions indicate Google is already associating FitMe Pro with these topics.
